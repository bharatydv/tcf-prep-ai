The full TCF Canada to CLB / NCLC chart (2026)

TCF Canada does not give you one overall score. It gives you four separate scores, and IRCC converts each one to a CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) level independently. In French-language contexts IRCC calls this same scale NCLC (Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens) — CLB and NCLC are the identical 1–12 ladder, just named differently depending on which official language the test was in.

Why your four scores do not match up (and why that is normal)

Open your TCF Canada attestation de résultats and you will see something like: Listening 480, Reading 465, Writing 11, Speaking 9. That is not a mistake. It is because listening and reading are machine-scored on a 0–699 scale (multiple choice, 39 questions each), while writing and speaking are human-graded on a 0–20 scale by trained examiners against descriptor criteria. Two completely different scales, two completely different shapes of number — but they can land on the exact same CLB level.

The one thing to actually pay attention to: listening and reading have different score thresholds for the same CLB level. Look at CLB 7 above — you need 458 for listening but only 453 for reading. It is a small gap, but it is exactly the kind of thing that trips people up when they are mentally rounding scores.

How much score do you need for CLB 7 in TCF Canada?

This is the number almost everyone preparing for Express Entry is actually searching for, so let us be precise:

  • Listening: 458 or higher (out of 699)
  • Reading: 453 or higher (out of 699)
  • Writing: 10 or 11 (out of 20)
  • Speaking: 10 or 11 (out of 20)

You need all four, not an average. This is the part that catches candidates off guard: you can score a strong 490 in listening and 480 in reading, feel great about your file, and still not qualify for CLB 7 overall if your speaking comes back at a 9. IRCC reads your weakest skill as your usable level for any requirement that asks for a minimum across all four abilities. A high listening score cannot rescue a low writing score.

Why CLB 7 specifically matters: it is the general benchmark for Express Entry’s French-language requirements, and NCLC 7 in all four skills is what unlocks the biggest French-language CRS bonus — up to 50 additional Comprehensive Ranking System points when combined with your English score — plus eligibility for French-language category-based draws, which have run with noticeably lower cutoffs than general draws.

How much score do you need for CLB 5 in TCF Canada?

CLB 5 is a lower bar than CLB 7, and comes up most often for candidates checking eligibility for specific programs, PGWP-linked requirements, or simply benchmarking early progress before pushing toward CLB 7.

  • Listening: 369–397
  • Reading: 375–405
  • Writing: exactly 6
  • Speaking: exactly 6

Notice how narrow the writing and speaking bands are at CLB 5 — a single point either side moves you a full level. That is worth knowing if you are close: a 5 in writing keeps you at CLB 4, while a 7 already pushes you into CLB 6.

CLB level, CRS points, and what actually changes as you climb

A quick mental model, since this is usually why someone is checking this chart in the first place:

  • Below CLB 4: generally too low to count meaningfully toward most economic immigration streams
  • CLB 5–6: meets the minimum for a number of programs, but well short of the French-bonus threshold
  • CLB 7 (all four skills): the real inflection point — unlocks the significant French-language CRS bonus and French category-based draw eligibility
  • CLB 8–9: further CRS gains, particularly valuable if you are also strong in English (bilingual candidates get an extra bump)
  • CLB 10+: near-native level; helpful for some provincial programs and citizenship timelines, but the marginal CRS gain over CLB 9 is smaller than the jump from CLB 6 to CLB 7

The practical takeaway for study planning: going from CLB 6 to CLB 7 is usually worth far more to your file than going from CLB 9 to CLB 10. If your time is limited, that is where to point it. Our NCLC 7 guide goes deeper on what that target looks like skill by skill.

How to actually check your CLB level from your TCF Canada result

  1. Open your official attestation de résultats (score report).
  2. Find your four separate scores — do not average them.
  3. Look up each score in its own column of the table above (listening is not read against the reading column, even though both go up to 699).
  4. Your usable overall level for any “minimum in all four skills” requirement is your lowest single score’s CLB level.
  5. Double-check the current numbers on IRCC’s official site before entering anything into your Express Entry profile — a wrong entry here is a documented source of application delays.

FAQs

How many marks do I need in TCF Canada for CLB 7?

You need 458+ in listening, 453+ in reading, and 10 or 11 in both writing and speaking. All four, not an average.

Is CLB 7 the same as NCLC 7?

Yes. NCLC is simply the French name for the same CLB scale IRCC uses for English tests. NCLC 7 = CLB 7 = the identical benchmark.

What TCF Canada score is CLB 5?

369–397 in listening, 375–405 in reading, and exactly 6 in both writing and speaking.

Does IRCC average my four TCF Canada scores?

No. Each of the four skills is converted to its own CLB level independently, and your file is judged against your weakest skill wherever a program asks for a minimum across all abilities.

Why is my listening score so much higher than my speaking score if I am at the same CLB level?

Because they are scored on completely different scales — listening and reading go up to 699 (machine-scored, multiple choice), while writing and speaking are scored out of 20 by human examiners. The scale size is different; the CLB meaning can still be identical.

How many CRS points does CLB 7 give me?

Reaching NCLC 7 in all four skills unlocks a meaningful French-language CRS bonus (up to 50 points combined with English ability under current Express Entry rules) and opens eligibility for French category-based draws. Always confirm the current point values on IRCC’s official CRS tool, as these have changed before.

Is it easier to get CLB 7 in TCF Canada or TEF Canada?

Both map to the identical CLB scale, so neither test is officially easier — but TEF spreads speaking and writing over a wider point range, which some candidates find gives more cushion for an off day, while TCF’s more compact 0–20 scale means a single point can shift a full level.

How long is my TCF Canada CLB result valid for?

Two years from your test date. After that, most programs require a fresh result.
