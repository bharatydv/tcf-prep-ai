The full TCF Canada to CLB / NCLC chart (2026)

TCF Canada doesn't give you one overall score. It gives you four separate scores, and IRCC converts each one to a CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) level independently. In French-language contexts, IRCC calls this same scale NCLC (Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens) — CLB and NCLC are the identical 1–12 ladder, just named differently depending on which official language the test was in.

Why your four scores don't match up (and why that's normal)

Open your TCF Canada attestation de résultats and you'll see something like: Listening 480, Reading 465, Writing 11, Speaking 9. That's not a mistake. It's because listening and reading are machine-scored on a 0–699 scale (multiple choice, 39 questions each), while writing and speaking are human-graded on a 0–20 scale by trained examiners against descriptor criteria. Two completely different scales, two completely different "shapes" of number — but they can land on the exact same CLB level.

The one thing to actually pay attention to: listening and reading have different score thresholds for the same CLB level. Look at CLB 7 above — you need 458 for listening but only 453 for reading. It's a small gap, but it's exactly the kind of thing that trips people up when they're mentally rounding scores.

How much score do you need for CLB 7 in TCF Canada?

This is the number almost everyone preparing for Express Entry is actually searching for, so let's be precise:

Listening: 458 or higher (out of 699)

Reading: 453 or higher (out of 699)

Writing: 10 or 11 (out of 20)

Speaking: 10 or 11 (out of 20)

You need all four, not an average. This is the part that catches candidates off guard: you can score a strong 490 in listening and 480 in reading, feel great about your file — and still not qualify for CLB 7 overall if your speaking comes back at a 9. IRCC reads your weakest skill as your usable level for any requirement that asks for a minimum across all four abilities. A high listening score cannot rescue a low writing score.

Why CLB 7 specifically matters: it's the general benchmark for Express Entry's French-language requirements, and NCLC 7 in all four skills is what unlocks the biggest French-language CRS bonus — up to 50 additional Comprehensive Ranking System points when combined with your English score, plus eligibility for French-language category-based draws, which have run with noticeably lower cutoffs than general draws.

How much score do you need for CLB 5 in TCF Canada?

CLB 5 is a lower bar than CLB 7, and comes up most often for candidates checking eligibility for specific programs, PGWP-linked requirements, or simply benchmarking early progress before pushing toward CLB 7.

Listening: 369–397

Reading: 375–405

Writing: exactly 6

Speaking: exactly 6

Notice how narrow the writing and speaking bands are at CLB 5 — a single point either side moves you a full level. That's worth knowing if you're close: a 5 in writing keeps you at CLB 4, while a 7 already pushes you into CLB 6.

CLB level, CRS points, and what actually changes as you climb

A quick mental model, since this is usually why someone is checking this chart in the first place:

Below CLB 4: generally too low to count meaningfully toward most economic immigration streams

CLB 5–6: meets the minimum for a number of programs, but well short of the French-bonus threshold

CLB 7 (all four skills): the real inflection point — unlocks the significant French-language CRS bonus and French category-based draw eligibility

CLB 8–9: further CRS gains, particularly valuable if you're also strong in English (bilingual candidates get an extra bump)

CLB 10+: near-native level; helpful for some provincial programs and citizenship timelines, but the marginal CRS gain over CLB 9 is smaller than the jump from CLB 6 to CLB 7

How to actually check your CLB level from your TCF Canada result

Open your official attestation de résultats (score report).

Find your four separate scores — don't average them.

Look up each score in its own column of the table above (listening ≠ reading, even though both go up to 699).

Your usable overall level for any "minimum in all four skills" requirement is your lowest single score's CLB level.

Double-check the current numbers on IRCC's official site before entering anything into your Express Entry profile — a wrong entry here is a documented source of application delays.

Worked example: reading a real result

Say your attestation shows: Listening 470, Reading 460, Writing 11, Speaking 9. Here's how to read that correctly:

Listening 470 → falls in the 458–502 band → CLB 7

Reading 460 → falls in the 453–498 band → CLB 7

Writing 11 → falls in the 10–11 band → CLB 7

Speaking 9 → falls in the 7–9 band → CLB 6

Three skills at CLB 7, one at CLB 6 — and for any program requiring CLB 7 across all four abilities, this file reads as CLB 6, full stop. This is exactly the scenario that catches strong candidates off guard: three genuinely excellent scores don't outweigh one that falls just short. If you're in this position, the fix is narrow and specific — you don't need to re-study everything, you need focused speaking practice on whichever task pattern is costing you those two or three points.

What to do if your skills are at different levels

This is the normal case, not the exception — very few candidates land on the exact same CLB level across all four skills on a first attempt. A few practical next steps depending on where the gap sits:

Gap is in Speaking: book a live 1:1 mock speaking interview for targeted, examiner-style feedback rather than more solo practice — speaking is the hardest skill to self-correct.

Gap is in Writing: get every attempt corrected against the real CLB criteria (task achievement, coherence, range, accuracy), not just checked for grammar mistakes.

Gap is in Listening or Reading: this is usually a pacing problem more than a comprehension one — practice under the real timer, not untimed, since 92 seconds per reading question changes your strategy once the clock is actually running.

All four are close but under CLB 7: focus study time evenly rather than picking one skill, since you're likely to cross the CLB 7 line on all four around the same time.

Frequently asked questions

How many marks do I need in TCF Canada for CLB 7?

You need 458+ in listening, 453+ in reading, and 10 or 11 in both writing and speaking. All four, not an average.

Is CLB 7 the same as NCLC 7?

Yes. NCLC is simply the French name for the same CLB scale IRCC uses for English tests. NCLC 7 = CLB 7 = the identical benchmark.

What TCF Canada score is CLB 5?

369–397 in listening, 375–405 in reading, and exactly 6 in both writing and speaking.

Does IRCC average my four TCF Canada scores?

No. Each of the four skills is converted to its own CLB level independently, and your file is judged against your weakest skill wherever a program asks for a minimum across all abilities.

Why is my listening score so much higher than my speaking score if I'm at the same CLB level?

Because they're scored on completely different scales — listening and reading go up to 699 (machine-scored, multiple choice), while writing and speaking are scored out of 20 by human examiners. The scale size is different; the CLB meaning can still be identical.

How many CRS points does CLB 7 give me?

Reaching NCLC 7 in all four skills unlocks a meaningful French-language CRS bonus (up to 50 points combined with English ability under current Express Entry rules) and opens eligibility for French category-based draws. Always confirm the current point values on IRCC's official CRS tool, as these have changed before.

Is it easier to get CLB 7 in TCF Canada or TEF Canada?

Both map to the identical CLB scale, so neither test is officially "easier" — but TEF spreads speaking and writing over a wider point range, which some candidates find gives more cushion for an off day, while TCF's more compact 0–20 scale means a single point can shift a full level.

How long is my TCF Canada CLB result valid for?

Two years from your test date. After that, most programs require a fresh result.

What's a good CLB score if I just want to know how I'm progressing, not for an application yet?

There's no universal "good" score outside the context of a specific program's requirement — the useful benchmark is your own weakest skill relative to CLB 7, since that's the threshold most Express Entry candidates are ultimately working toward.
